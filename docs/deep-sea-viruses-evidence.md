# Deep-sea viruses: evidence reviewed on 2026-10-08

Primary research: Hou, Zhang and Sun, Nature Communications, published 29 September 2026. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78161-0. Read the full 17-page peer-reviewed accepted manuscript, not only the abstract. Publisher states that the final version of record is forthcoming.

Sampling: Methods, sample collection; 53 usable sediment samples at 14 stations from COMRA DY79 in 2023 and DY88 in 2024. Seven previously published metagenomes bring the analysis to 60 samples. A multiple corer collected sediment; the survey sequenced DNA, not RNA. Four analysed sediment intervals: 0–2, 4–6, 8–10 and 14–16 cm below the seabed.

Depth verification: original Supplementary Data 1, rows 3–55, column E, 53 newly collected sample records. Numeric strings parsed as numbers. Minimum 5169 m, maximum 5285 m below sea level. Do not attribute this range to all older samples, which include shallower stations.

Catalogue: Results, establishment of CCZV; 14,181 viral sequences longer than 5 kb, grouped into 11,742 vOTUs at 95% ANI and minimum 85% alignment coverage. 11,662 vOTUs (99.3%, rounded in source) did not cluster with reference sequences. 519 complete genomes. Most representatives are not complete genomes. vOTUs are operational species-level groups, not a count of newly ICTV-approved species.

Hosts: virus-host interactions results; 482 vOTUs (4.1%) linked computationally to reconstructed microbial genomes. These are predicted associations, not cultured infection tests. No evidence of human infection or a new human health threat is presented.

Functional interpretation: candidate metabolic genes and possible metal-resistance gene exchange indicate hypotheses, not measured ecosystem rates. Read Martin and colleagues, Nature Microbiology 2025, DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-025-02095-4, for annotation limitations. Background viral recycling context: Danovaro and colleagues, Nature 2008, https://www.nature.com/articles/nature07268.

Mining: NOAA 2025 science assessment, https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/69973/noaa_69973_DS1.pdf. The viral study is a baseline, not a mining disturbance experiment or proof of recovery. Do not add unverified current regulatory claims.
