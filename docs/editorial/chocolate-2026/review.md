## Evidence and scope

- Zarrillo et al. (2018), DOI 10.1038/s41559-018-0697-x, supports evidence of cacao use about 5,300 years ago in southeastern Ecuador. This does not establish the first individual user or a specific recipe. Older exclusively Mesoamerican origin accounts are not repeated.
- Smithsonian supports cultural and European history; Afoakwa's Chocolate Science and Technology excerpt supports industrial milestones. Daniel Peter is dated to the 1870s to avoid conflating experiments with commercial milestones. Company histories are attributed, not treated as independent health evidence.
- ICCO processing guidance, fermentation research and Royal Society of Chemistry educational material distinguish fermentation, roasting, refining, conching and tempering. White chocolate contains cocoa butter. Ruby is a manufacturer's development, not a new species.
- ICCO August 2026 bulletin estimates 2024/25 production at 4.733 million tonnes and withholds 2025/26 estimates. The article identifies the season, edition and provisional nature rather than combining different revisions or calendar-year data.
- COSMOS original randomized trial: 21,442 older US adults, median 3.6 years, nonsignificant primary cardiovascular endpoint. Secondary cardiovascular mortality signal is qualified. Extract is distinguished from chocolate bars; Mars Edge support is disclosed. NIH supports the nutritional caution.
- NORC's hazardous child-labor estimate is dated 2018/19 and limited to cocoa-growing areas in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana. It is not presented as a current worldwide total. The photographed Solomon Islands farmer is explicitly unrelated to those findings.
- FAO agroforestry reporting and fermentation research support the forward-looking discussion without speculative deadlines or promises.
- No invented quotations, first-person reporting, medical credentials or em dashes. Internal coffee link is routed to the matching language.
