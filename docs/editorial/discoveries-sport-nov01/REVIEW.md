## Verified editorial evidence

- Dinosaur: Funston and colleagues' Nature Communications paper, published 10 October 2026, describes Infernogallus jamesi and the unusual jaw organisation supporting soft-plant-food processing, probably including substantial fleshy fruit. ROM and Stony Brook's 28 October announcements document Hell Creek provenance, caenagnathid membership, toothlessness and approximate adult mass. Diet is an anatomical inference, not a fossil fruit photographed in the mouth. The 2022 eLife Jeholornis study prevents an inaccurate claim that this is the first fruit consumption anywhere in the dinosaur family tree. Proposed seed dispersal is not presented as a demonstrated mechanism, distance or ecological impact.
- Wild cat: Lescroart and colleagues' Current Biology study uses whole genomes from 38 cats, including eight museum specimens, to resolve five tiger-cat species, including newly described Leopardus tilcayo from Bolivia. PUCRS and the National Geographic Society provide primary institutional accounts. A new scientific name is distinguished from the appearance of a newly evolved animal. The century claim concerns a formal description with a new name, not an absence of taxonomic revisions. Four previously proposed species are not described as four additional completely new discoveries. No unsupported wild population count or current conservation category is invented.
- Amber insect: Fabrikant, Fu and Davranoglou's Insects paper was published 29 July 2026. It describes Cretodorus noonoo as an extinct planthopper and investigates corrugated mouthparts using microscopy and micro-CT. Tel Aviv University's account explains the nickname and unusual feeding structure. Plant-fluid feeding through bark fissures is a functional interpretation; the article does not claim blood feeding, demonstrated pollination or recovered DNA. Shi and colleagues' 2012 zircon-dating paper supports the approximate Kachin amber age. The age of the deposit is distinguished from the 2026 species description.
- Sperm whales: Miller and colleagues' 2008 Current Biology paper documents shallow vertical drift-resting and reduced responsiveness. The St Andrews account supplies 59 tagged whales, 562 recording hours and the interpretation limits of 7.1% of tag time. Freymond, Burslem and Miller's 2026 Journal of Experimental Biology study matches models to depth profiles from 10 of 42 Norwegian tagged whales, predominantly resting head-down, and supports buoyancy regulation through bubble release. This explains a mechanism without claiming a complete evolutionary answer. Brain activity and sleep stages were not measured by these movement tags. Oliveira and colleagues' 2022 vessel-exposure study supports the discussion of disturbed rest.
- Tennis: the official Erste Bank Open final report dated 1 November 2026 confirms Carlos Alcaraz beat Alex de Minaur 6-1, 6-4 in 87 minutes, his first Vienna title, with no sets lost during the week. Official semi-final reports confirm Alcaraz beat Medvedev 6-4, 7-6(2), and de Minaur beat Zverev 6-4, 1-6, 7-6(4). The official entry announcement confirms the 15 October late addition and previous 2021 appearance. The ATP calendar confirms ATP 500 status and hard courts. The final result was verified before drafting and publication; no invented quotes, serve percentages, prize money, ranking changes or title totals are added.

Arabic tennis score runs use Unicode LTR isolation within RTL prose, metadata and FAQs. A browser range measurement exposed reversed score digits in the initial rendering; the final suite explicitly measures that 6 precedes 1 visually within the 6–1 score at every breakpoint. The numeric result is unchanged.

All prose is original. All four languages preserve scientific qualifications, evidence, source order, internal destinations, section structure and FAQ subjects. Literal and encoded em dashes are excluded under AGENTS.md.
