## Scientific checks

- First mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell, not first cloned animal or sheep. Birth 5 July 1996, public announcement 22 February 1997, Nature publication 27 February 1997.
- Adult mammary donor was a six-year-old Finn Dorset; egg and gestational contributors were Scottish Blackface sheep. Nuclear and mitochondrial genetic contributions distinguished.
- 277 fused/reconstructed egg-cell combinations, 29 embryos transferred to 13 recipients, one live lamb. Not 277 pregnancies or 276 dead lambs. Original 1997 paper linked; the contemporaneous National Bioethics Advisory Commission report corroborates experimental numbers. Developmental Biology/NCBI and Roslin explain the mechanism.
- Team credit includes the five original paper authors, Campbell's role, embryology, veterinary work and animal care.
- Six naturally conceived offspring, first Bonnie in 1998. Arthritis in 2001, lung disease associated with JSRV, euthanasia 14 February 2003 at six and a half. Roslin and NMS agree on the central chronology; a conflicting age for arthritis in a Roslin FAQ was not used.
- Short telomeres do not establish whole-organism premature aging. The 2016 study involved 13 surviving clones; survivor selection and limited cohort size are explicit. The 2017 skeletal study did not erase the clinical diagnosis of arthritis; unavailable records and limits of radiography are stated.
- Nuclear-transfer stem-cell research distinguished from reproductive cloning, gene editing and embryo selection. Gurdon/Yamanaka credited separately. ISSCR professional guidelines not presented as universal law. No therapeutic promises or speculative deadlines.
