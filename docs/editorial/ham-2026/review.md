# Ham publication review

16 October 2026. Article 274, Science. English original approximately 1,660 words including headings and linked labels; 34 corresponding blocks in EN/FR/AR/ES.

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- Mercury-Redstone 2: 31 January 1961, explicitly suborbital. First chimpanzee in space, not first animal, not an orbital flight.
- NASA historical reference gives approximately 16 minutes 39 seconds, 157 statute miles altitude and 422 statute miles downrange. Converted distances are rounded. Primary accounts use slightly different rounded figures; no false precision is implied.
- NASA historical account documents lever conditioning with food and electric-shock avoidance, two errors in 50 right-hand responses, correct left-hand responses and 14.7 g reentry. Correct behavior is not treated as evidence of comfort or consent.
- Project review supports propellant-depletion abort and approximately 110 nautical mile overshoot. Recovery succeeded despite spacecraft flooding; Ham's enclosure protected him.
- MR-BD on 24 March and Shepard on 5 May distinguish scientific evidence from the exaggerated claim that Ham alone enabled human flight. Gagarin, Enos and Glenn chronology is explicit.
- Cameroonian origin and July 1959 Holloman transfer are attributed. Conflicting exact birth dates are not harmonized by invention. Death stated as January 1983; Smithsonian reports heart disease. No unsupported inner thoughts, dialogue or claims about facial expressions.
- Smithsonian archival record supports September 1980 transfer after 17 years at National Zoo.
- Laika and Félicette comparisons link to existing published routes, as does Animal Testing. No duplicate articles created.
- Thirteen sources linked in each edition, with localized source labels and matching citation URLs. No fabricated direct quotation or credential.
