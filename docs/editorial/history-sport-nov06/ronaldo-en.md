Cristiano Ronaldo announced his retirement from Portugal’s national team on 5 November 2026, closing a chapter that began in August 2003. FIFA’s report records 234 senior appearances and 146 goals, both men’s international records. The distinction is essential: this is an international retirement. FIFA says the 41-year-old will continue his club career with Al-Nassr in Saudi Arabia. The number seven is leaving one stage of football, rather than announcing that he will stop playing altogether. [FIFA: Ronaldo announces Portugal retirement, 5 November 2026](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/articles/cristiano-ronaldo-portugal-retirement)

## What has been confirmed?

UEFA’s record page, updated on 5 November, independently gives the same totals and announcement date. Those figures describe the senior Portugal record reported at the announcement, not his club statistics or a combined career total. Keeping the categories separate avoids a familiar distortion: a national-team farewell should not be headlined or illustrated as the end of every professional commitment. This article was published on 6 November, reporting the previous day’s announcement rather than presenting it as a new statement issued today. ## The statement behind the farewell

In the video statement reproduced by FourFourTwo, Ronaldo framed the decision around returning the national shirt and captain’s armband and making room for the future. His account is the public explanation he chose to give. It does not establish every private conversation behind the decision. Claims about dressing-room conflict, selection pressure or a particular person forcing his departure require their own evidence; the confirmed announcement does not license turning speculation into a motive. ## From a 2003 debut to lasting records

Associated Press also confirms the 23-year span and the 234-appearance, 146-goal record. Longevity gives those numbers their meaning: the total represents repeated selection and scoring across changing teams and tournaments. [Associated Press: Ronaldo’s international career by the numbers](https://apnews.com/article/94845372ae5b9ca57b1424a0595f7810)

UEFA dates his first international goals record-breaking performance to September 2021, when two goals against the Republic of Ireland took him beyond Ali Daei’s mark. His scoring did not begin with his debut year: the organization records goals in 23 successive calendar years from 2004 through 2026. An appearance record and a scoring streak measure different achievements. Neither reduces a career to one match, and neither needs an invented final scene to communicate its scale. [UEFA: Ronaldo’s international record, updated 5 November 2026](https://www.uefa.com/news/026a-1297500e1b34-a17bbbcad258-1000--ronaldo-all-time-top-scorer/)

## A career that also delivered trophies

FIFA identifies the team honours at the centre of his Portugal legacy: EURO 2016 and Nations League titles in 2019 and 2025. These are collective achievements, not trophies won by a player alone. The same report credits him with scoring at six World Cups, from 2006 through 2026. Together, tournament appearances, records and team successes explain why the announcement reaches beyond supporters of his current club. They do not erase the roles of teammates, coaches and successive generations around him.
