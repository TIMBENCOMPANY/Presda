# Laika editorial and production review

Reviewed 16 October 2026. Article ID 270. Science. Four original localized editions at `laika-first-dog-orbit-never-came-home`.

## Evidence decisions

- NASA's modern mission account supports 3 November 1957, first animal in orbit, no recovery and survival of only a few hours. Earlier NASA educational material repeating survival for days or calling Laika the first animal in space was not used for those claims.
- NASA's animal-flight history establishes the distinction between suborbital and orbital experiments and the later return of Belka and Strelka. Its questionable precise dates for earlier dog flights are not repeated.
- ESA supplies Space Race context. Smithsonian supplies preparation and contemporary objections. Anatoly Zak's Smithsonian Air & Space investigation explains fragmentary telemetry and the 1957 report released in 2011. The article does not claim an exact death time or that 2002 was the first disclosure: Gazenko's 1993 acknowledgment is included.
- The NASA historical technical compilation is cited for life-support equipment, not its outdated survival duration. No imagined thoughts, dialogue, last moments or voluntary sacrifice are attributed to Laika.
- The 3Rs are identified as a later framework associated with 1959, not a law applicable to the 1957 mission.
- All editions have the same nine reference URLs, equivalent claims and a localized contextual link to Animal Testing. No fabricated quotations or expert medical review claim.

## Images and commercial reuse

1. Supplied hero: AI artistic reconstruction, labeled in all four editions. Not evidence of Laika's appearance, original equipment or mission conditions.
2. Laika portrait: Wikimedia `File:Laika_in_1957.jpg`, Museum of Cosmonautics / Moscow Main Archive, published by Mos.ru. File record explicitly licenses CC BY 4.0 (Mos.ru). Visible interactive Mos.ru credit, license and record links included. Resizing and WebP conversion disclosed. Original 1600 × 1106.
3. Sputnik 2 orbital photograph: Wikimedia `File:Photograph_of_Sputnik_2_taken_from_Patrick_AFB_in_March_1958.jpg`. U.S. Air Force personnel / Air Force Missile Test Center. U.S. government public domain. Original 659 × 399, never enlarged. Caption makes clear that March 1958 was after Laika's death.
4. Authentic mission-era document: NASA-prepared satellite chronology, Appendix F, printed page 86, PDF page 93, in U.S. Senate Report 1014, 16 December 1959. NTRS record 20150018617 permits public use. The page explicitly identifies NASA as preparer; public-domain federal work under 17 USC 105. Full page rotated for reading, resized and converted. Caption distinguishes a contemporary American summary from a Soviet flight log and the spacecraft lifetime from survival.

Training-photo limitation: searched Commons and historical collections but did not establish suitable commercial rights and reliable identification for a separate training photograph. Rejected `Лайка (собака-космонавт)1.jpg` and related image because anonymous 1957/1960s provenance did not substantiate its Russian public-domain template. Also rejected generic NASA-hosted Energia images as third-party material without a reuse grant. No AI substitute or mislabeled museum replica inserted. The verified preflight portrait accompanies selection and training coverage.

Rejected Astrogram page: the 1959 table was a third-party reprint, so NASA hosting alone was not treated as a rights grant. The Senate document used instead identifies its table as prepared by NASA.
