Some fish announce themselves with speed. This one makes an entrance by taking its time. Close to the seabed, a broad-bodied animal props itself on its fins, apparently wearing a startling pair of red lips. The red-lipped batfish is real, but the familiar joke about lipstick can distract from its more interesting achievement: using a fish’s equipment to move through a world of sand, rock and small prey.

## A fish built for the bottom

Its scientific name is Ogcocephalus darwini. Galapagos Conservation Trust describes it as a bottom dweller around the Galápagos, living on sandy reef bottoms and the ocean floor. Its brownish-grey upper surface is much less theatrical than its mouth. Seen from above against the substrate, the body offers a different visual story from the close-up portraits that made the species an internet favourite. [Galapagos Conservation Trust: red-lipped batfish](https://galapagosconservation.org.uk/species/red-lipped-batfish/)

## Walking with fins, not feet

The apparent legs are fins. They support the animal as it perches and moves along the bottom; they are not the limbs of a land animal returning to the sea. It can also swim. Calling it a walking fish is a useful description of how it moves, provided that the phrase does not become a claim that it has abandoned swimming or evolved human-like knees and feet. [Charles Darwin Foundation: Galápagos species database](https://datazone.darwinfoundation.org/en/checklist/?species=7663)

## A small predator with a lure

A structure on its head, called an illicium, is thought to help lure prey. The conservation profile lists small fish, crustaceans and molluscs among its food. This is a small predator’s life, not a miniature seafloor grazing scene. The distinction also matters when interpreting an illustration: a pose can suggest an activity, but a still image cannot establish what an animal was hunting or how it caught it. [Galapagos Conservation Trust: proposed lure function](https://galapagosconservation.org.uk/species/red-lipped-batfish/) [Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute: Galápagos batfish](https://biogeodb.stri.si.edu/sftep/en/thefishes/species/776)

## The lips leave an open question

Bright lips are an observable feature; their evolutionary function is a separate question. The source profile does not establish that the colour attracts a mate. Repeating that appealing explanation as a proven fact would go beyond the evidence reviewed here. The honest answer is less tidy and more interesting: noticing a striking feature is the beginning of an investigation, not its conclusion. This fish needs no invented certainty to be extraordinary.
