Assane Diop's greatest asset has always been the story people tell about him. In Lupin Part 4, that reputation becomes a trap. Omar Sy returns as the gentleman thief inspired by Maurice Leblanc's fictional Arsène Lupin, in a new chapter that asks what happens when somebody else starts borrowing the legend he has built.

Netflix released all eight episodes on October 23, 2026. Its updated announcement confirms that the complete fourth part is available, rather than a weekly rollout or an unconfirmed release rumor. This guide covers the premise, people behind the production and early reception as of October 24, without revealing the copycat's identity or the ending. [Netflix's release announcement](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/lupin-part-4-release-date-photos-news)

## A four-year jump, and a thief trying to stop

According to Netflix's official synopsis, four years have passed inside the story. Assane remains in prison and wants to finish his sentence, then keep a promise to his son by joining their family in Senegal. That plan is disrupted when someone announces simultaneous museum robberies under the Lupin name. The crimes take place, and suspicion falls on the man who was supposed to be safely behind bars. [Official Part 4 synopsis](https://media.netflix.com/en/only-on-netflix/80994082)

The premise reverses the usual pleasure of a master-thief story. A reputation for being able to escape anything makes confinement a less convincing alibi. It also gives the family promise a practical cost: clearing his name threatens to drag Assane back into the behavior he wants to abandon. That is the dramatic question established by the official setup, not a clue to how the season resolves it.

## Omar Sy returns with a familiar ensemble

The announced cast includes Sy alongside Ludivine Sagnier, Antoine Gouy, Soufiane Guerrab and Shirine Boutella, with Théo Christine and Laïka Blanc-Francard also joining the lineup. Returning viewers know Sagnier as Claire, Gouy as Benjamin, Guerrab as Guédira and Boutella as Belkacem. The relationships around Assane remain essential to a story that would otherwise risk becoming only a sequence of elaborate thefts. [Cast announcement and returning roles](https://www.tvline.com/news/lupin-renewed-part-4-cast-episode-count-netflix-1235448788/)

Sy's position extends beyond the central performance. Netflix credits him and Louis Leterrier as showrunners, with Gaumont producing in collaboration with Carrousel Studios. The series was created by George Kay in collaboration with François Uzan. Those credits matter because a star's return and a star's creative responsibility are different things; here the lead actor is also involved in shaping the project. [Netflix's creative-team credits](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/articles/lupin-part-4-release-date-photos-news)

## How the fourth part reached the screen

Production was publicly announced in Paris in May 2025. Netflix's statement described the involvement of Gaumont and Carrousel and Sy's desire to give the new episodes greater ambition. The filming announcement and the October 2026 release are separate milestones: an older article saying the series was in production should not be mistaken for the current availability notice. [Netflix's May 2025 production statement](https://www.pressparty.com/pg/newsdesk/Netflix/view/460810/)

The fourth part also arrives with a different scale of storytelling to its opening installment: eight episodes, compared with the first part's five. More room can allow consequences to develop, but it also places greater demands on pacing. A heist series needs the audience to understand enough of a plan to appreciate the deception, while keeping enough hidden to make the revelation satisfying. Greater spectacle does not automatically solve that balancing act. [Netflix's first-part episode guide](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/lupin)

## The global success is real, but the numbers need labels

Netflix's most-popular non-English television chart records 99.5 million views for Lupin Part 1 and 68.4 million for Part 2. Those are measurements over each part's first 91 days, not lifetime individual viewers and not opening-weekend results for Part 4. Netflix calculates views from hours watched divided by runtime. Adding the two totals would not produce a count of distinct people, since the same subscriber may have watched both parts. [Netflix's official chart and methodology](https://www.netflix.com/tudum/top10/most-popular/tv-non-english)

The figures demonstrate substantial international reach for a French-language series. They do not guarantee that the new episodes will match it. Comparisons between launches should use the same viewing window and metric; an old household count, a modern view total and a social-media trend describe different things. At this publication's October 24 check, PRESDA had not verified an official Part 4 launch-view total and is not assigning one.

## Early reviews praise Sy, while some viewers question the technology

At the October 24 check, Rotten Tomatoes displayed 100% positive reviews from five critics for Part 4. That small sample describes the proportion of reviews judged positive, not a perfect average score. The page showed fewer than 50 audience ratings and no displayed audience percentage. It is too early to treat either the critic sample or the available viewer comments as a settled public verdict. [Dated reception snapshot on Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/lupin/s04)

The visible audience comments included objections to implausible technology and a perceived loss of the earlier installments' grounded ingenuity. Those are individual responses, not a representative survey. Read alongside the positive critic sample, they identify a useful point of debate: whether expanding the action strengthens the escapism or makes the tricks feel less earned. Nothing in a handful of comments establishes what most subscribers think. [Early audience responses](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/lupin/s04)

## A literary identity built for a streaming audience

The distinction between Assane Diop and Arsène Lupin is central. Sy plays a modern character who draws inspiration from Leblanc's creation, not a literal transplant of the original thief into the present. That structure lets the series use the pleasure of literary recognition without making familiarity with the books a requirement. Netflix identifies the show's country of origin as France and presents it as a mix of thriller, mystery and action. [Netflix's title information](https://media.netflix.com/en/only-on-netflix/80994082)

For a returning viewer, the interest lies in what remains recognizable beneath the escalation: a performer controlling attention, a plan that depends on how others see him, and family relationships that prevent cleverness from being consequence-free. The premise makes reputation both a resource and a liability. That tension is more durable than any particular gadget, disguise or escape route.
