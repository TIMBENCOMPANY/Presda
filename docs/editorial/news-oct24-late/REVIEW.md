### Castillo de Huarmey

- Tomczyk and colleagues, Journal of Anthropological Archaeology 82 (June 2026), 101767, DOI 10.1016/j.jaa.2026.101767, open access: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278416526000206
- Dartmouth, May 20, 2026: https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2026/05/peruvian-hairless-dogs-discovered-ancient-site
- Andina researcher interview, June 27: https://andina.pe/ingles/noticia-peru-castillo-huarmey-find-reveals-peruvian-hairless-dogs-ritual-role-1081301.aspx
- Andina research account, April 26: https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-perro-sin-pelo-hallazgo-castillo-huarmey-revela-su-desempeno-la-cultura-wari-1072301.aspx

This is a feature explaining a 2026 study, not a claim of a fresh October excavation. The 341 count is bone specimens, with a separately recorded nearly complete male. Dartmouth describes a minimum of 19 dogs plus that male. The PIT-211 calibrated range is 688–870 CE; roughly 1,200 years is an approximation, not an exact or universal date for every specimen. The identification of three probable hairless dogs is anatomical, not an ancient-DNA pedigree. The paper's more cautious abstract is followed despite firmer wording elsewhere. Natural preservation is distinguished from deliberate embalming. The shallow-pit male had no funerary offerings; missing lower forelimbs do not establish ritual amputation. Cinnabar and human burial associations do not establish a universal sacrifice practice. Dietary isotope signals are not exact menus; intentional feeding and scavenging remain alternatives. Structured breeding and pet status are not asserted as proven.
