# Collecting, Margot Robbie and diamonds publication review

Reviewed October 22, 2026. Articles were authored and translated sequentially in that order. The source manuscripts, localized metadata and generation script are retained in this directory.

## Editorial evidence

- Collecting distinguishes Belk's extended-self framework from universal psychological claims. Southampton nostalgia research is not presented as evidence that buying objects treats illness. Berridge and Robinson's reward research does not establish a predictable dopamine response in collectors. APA and IOCDF definitions distinguish hoarding disorder by distress and impaired functioning, not collection size. FTC material supports the discussion of collectible investment risks.
- Robbie uses ABC reporting, distributor production notes, Academy records, company announcements and current film sources. LuckyChap's original cofounders are identified. Acting nominations and producing credits are distinguished. Barbie box office is not personal income or net profit. Announced Monopoly and Sims projects are not described as completed releases. The 2025 and 2026 films are supported by distributor sources and Reuters reporting. No speculative salary or net-worth figures are included.
- Diamonds separates geology from marketing, historical supply control from current competition, and the 2004 industrial-diamond antitrust case from jewellery retail. GIA supports history, grading and laboratory-grown material explanations. De Beers' first-half 2026 realized rough price is explicitly company-specific and affected by sales mix, not a global retail price index. Kimberley Process scope and FTC environmental-marketing concerns are described with their limits.

Full source URLs are preserved in the English manuscripts and article references. Translated paragraphs inherit the corresponding citations. Internal links point to existing published articles.
