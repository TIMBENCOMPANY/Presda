# Science and AI publication review, 1 November 2026

Three original explainers, twelve complete editions in English, French, Arabic and Spanish. Production baseline: b3aace91ab248e513f9ce7d23dce12906c17a605. Publication is explicitly requested as one batch.

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- Zebra stripes: the 2019 PLOS ONE experiments distinguish approaches from successful fly landings and include patterned horse coats. The 2023 Bristol research supports high contrast and small dark elements while testing, rather than presuming, optical mechanisms. The 2014 Nature Communications comparison supports an association with biting-fly pressure. The 2016 predator-vision model and 2018 barrel cooling experiment are described with their methodological limits. No complete insect protection, settled single optical mechanism or definitive rejection of every temperature-related function is claimed.
- Traffic lights: FHWA distinguishes established adaptive control from machine learning and explains the importance of detection, maintenance and existing signal quality. The original Carnegie Mellon SURTRAC paper identifies a nine-intersection before/after pilot, not a randomised citywide trial. Travel time, waiting time and modelled emissions are kept separate. Google Green Light is attributed to its provider, including engineer review and the difference between potential reductions in stops and a reduction in overall congestion. Safety, accessibility, privacy and evaluation recommendations are editorial analysis, not invented deployment findings.
- Antarctica: NSIDC distinguishes floating shelves and their seawater cavities from grounded continental ice. BAS Bedmap3 is a measured geophysical reconstruction, not a photograph of caverns or architecture. The 2014 Nature Lake Whillans paper documents microbial life in a sampled subglacial lake. BAS accounts distinguish the 2018 Ekström samples reported in 2021 and the 2025 A-84 expedition after calving from George VI. The sea-level explanation distinguishes already floating ice from the grounded ice it restrains.
- The Antarctic ruins are fictional AI illustration. That disclosure appears in every language's image caption, alt text, opening paragraph and FAQ. No lost city, underwater archaeology, whale-filled sealed lake or authentic research photograph is claimed.
- All twelve editions have matching structure, scientific qualifiers, source URLs, internal destinations and FAQ subjects. Literal and encoded em dashes are excluded under AGENTS.md.

## Supplied images and design

- The three approved JPEGs are converted once to descriptive WebP files, retaining their native 1280x720, 1280x721 and 1280x720 pixel geometry. No crop, resize, image generation or replacement is performed. Source and final SHA-256 fingerprints, dimensions, filenames and file sizes are recorded in manifest.json.
- Every original WebP is below 200 KB and exceeds Google's 1200-pixel large-preview width. The established pipeline creates responsive derivatives without upscaling.
- The originals contain no EXIF, IPTC or XMP metadata. New descriptive XMP credits supplied AI artwork and identifies the exact English article URL. Unknown creator, copyright and licence are not invented. Visible captions and ImageObject credits disclose AI generation.
- Bruno's shared full-width layout, typography, headline colors, date, deck and reading-time fitter are retained. The corrected natural-height full-image desktop behavior from the previous three articles is reused. Its scoped class is renamed to article-hero--full-image without changing its rules. Mobile keeps the complete image above readable copy. The supplied originals are near 16:9, matching the 1672x941 reference geometry without falsely claiming that native resolution.
