## Sources and editorial decisions

- Tigers: PBS Nature's Tiger Island descriptions and the official encounter transcript support an observation in western Nepal involving Goma, her two cubs, two additional cubs and Jugini nearby. This is described as apparent care, not proof of a permanent nursery, universal cooperation or a measured survival advantage. Documentary evidence is explicitly distinguished from a peer-reviewed population study. The two broadcasts are dated October 21 and 28, 2026. Smithsonian, the IUCN Cat Specialist Group and WWF supply developmental and conservation context. The different independence ranges remain attributed to their respective sources. No invented interviews, quotations, first-ever claims or precise filming date.
- Al-Khwarizmi: St Andrews' MacTutor biography and Sonja Brentjes' biographical reference establish the mathematical setting and biographical uncertainties. The MAA's historical manuscript and completing-the-square resources support the six equation types, rhetorical algebra and manuscript transmission. Bodleian and British Library catalogues document surviving copies. Birth dates are approximate and birthplace is not asserted. The contested institutional meaning of the House of Wisdom is acknowledged. The worked example uses explicitly modern notation; the additional negative solution is explained in terms of different historical domains. The article does not call him the inventor of zero or every algorithm. MAA's legacy pages are indexed and retrievable through search, although direct browser fetching intermittently returned an origin error.
- Copernicus: the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Glasgow and Oklahoma university collections, Cambridge's history-of-science material, ESA and NASA support the biography, 1543 publication, retrograde-motion explanation, limited contemporary evidence, later reception and Kepler's correction of circular orbits. The story that he received the book on his deathbed is not treated as established. He is not portrayed as the first person ever to consider heliocentrism, a martyr, or the discoverer of the Sun's position at the centre of the entire universe.
- Original explanatory passages connect the sources without fabricated expertise or quotations. Matching citations accompany the translated sections. Translation review is truthfully credited to Codex; no invented human reviewer or academic credentials. No artificial FAQs.

All three use Giordano Bruno's existing shared hero without fit overrides. This preserves Bruno's behaviour, including any aspect-ratio crop inherent in his edge-to-edge desktop frame, following the user's earlier explicit preference. No extra zoom, image edits or global style changes are introduced.
