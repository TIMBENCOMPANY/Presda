# Sex education publication review

Reviewed 14 October 2026. Lifestyle, four complete editions, 39 matching content blocks. English feature approximately 1,840 words including headings and citations.

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- WHO, UNESCO and UNICEF's 2018 technical guidance is identified as a joint synthesis, not three independent trials.
- Cochrane's 2016 review: eight trials, 55,157 participants. Knowledge, self-reported behavior and biological outcomes distinguished; education alone is not promised to prevent pregnancy or infection.
- 85% of 155 countries refers to the 2021 policy survey, not children reached or current implementation quality.
- WHO's 374 million figure refers to new infections with four curable STIs in 2020, not unique individuals or this year's total.
- Contraception is distinguished from STI protection. Emergency contraception is distinguished from abortion. Individual treatment decisions are referred to qualified professionals.
- Childhood education covers body vocabulary, privacy and help-seeking, without adult instruction. Responsibility for protection remains with adults and institutions.
- Cultural and religious perspectives are treated as diverse. Regional UNICEF materials are examples, not comparative impact studies.
- General digital-misinformation research is not represented as evidence of a platform causing sexual behavior.
- All cited source URLs, section order and internal links preserved across translations; reference labels localized.
- Supplied hero is labeled AI-generated editorial illustration; diagrams are explicitly not medical references. Original image preserved with generated responsive derivatives.
