AC Milan and Inter have unveiled Generali Stadium as their planned future home, placing a name and architectural vision around one of Italian football's most consequential building projects. The announcement of 29 October 2026 concerns a design and development plan, not the opening of a completed arena. Supporters have been shown what the clubs intend to build; several major steps still separate that vision from the first match. [Inter's official announcement](https://www.inter.it/en/news/inter-milan-generali-stadium-new-milano-stadium)

That distinction matters in a city where a stadium is both a sporting workplace and a shared landmark. A new venue has to function for two clubs, rival supporter cultures, surrounding neighborhoods and a commercial operation that extends well beyond match day. The most useful way to read the announcement is to separate the design, the property transaction, the sponsorship and the approvals still ahead.

## What has actually been unveiled?

The joint announcement describes a stadium of approximately 71,500 seats, designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA. Its visual features include X-shaped structural frames and a double-curved roof. The proposed bowl has two tiers, with steep end sections intended to concentrate the atmosphere created by the two sets of supporters. These are announced design characteristics, rather than measurements independently checked inside a finished building. [AC Milan's official project statement](https://www.acmilan.com/en/news/articles/club/2026-10-29/ac-milan-and-fc-internazionale-milano-unveil-generali-stadium-a-new-home-for-the-fans-a-new-landmark-for-milano)

### A shared stadium is not a shared identity

For Milan and Inter, sharing a physical venue does not dissolve the distinction between the clubs. Stadium planning has to accommodate separate match-day identities within common infrastructure: circulation, access, servicing and the spectator bowl. The test will be whether that arrangement works for ordinary ticket holders as well as premium customers. An impressive exterior alone cannot answer questions about sightlines, queues or the experience of arriving and leaving.

## When could the first match take place?

The clubs expect construction to begin in 2028 and target the 2031/32 season for the first match, subject to the necessary approvals. The scheme sits within a wider regeneration area of around 281,000 square metres. Neither the timetable nor that area should be misread: the former is a target with conditions, while the latter refers to the broader development rather than the playing surface or seating bowl alone. [Official timetable and development scope](https://www.inter.it/en/news/inter-milan-generali-stadium-new-milano-stadium)

### Ownership and permission are different milestones

An earlier step came on 5 November 2025, when the clubs announced completion of the acquisition of the San Siro property, including the Meazza stadium and surrounding land, through Stadio San Siro S.p.A. That transaction established a property basis for the project. It did not by itself demonstrate that every construction approval had been issued or that the proposed opening date was guaranteed. [AC Milan's acquisition announcement](https://www.acmilan.com/en/news/articles/club/2025-11-05/ac-milan-and-fc-internazionale-milano-complete-the-acquisition-of-the-san-siro-great-urban-function)

The acquisition release named Goldman Sachs and J.P. Morgan as co-lead arrangers, alongside Banco BPM and BPER in the financing. Their involvement is information about the transaction's financing structure, not a published final bill for the entire future stadium. Property costs, construction expenditure, borrowing terms and sponsorship income belong to different financial categories. Combining them into one spectacular headline number would obscure rather than explain the economics. [Official acquisition and financing statement](https://www.acmilan.com/en/news/articles/club/2025-11-05/ac-milan-and-fc-internazionale-milano-complete-the-acquisition-of-the-san-siro-great-urban-function)

## What does the Generali name mean?

Generali is the naming partner for the new venue and an official insurance partner of both clubs. The insurer's announcement establishes a commercial relationship around the stadium and the teams. The reviewed announcements do not provide a naming-rights fee or contract duration, so neither can responsibly be presented as a verified figure. [Generali's partnership announcement](https://www.generali.com/media/News/2026/Generali-becomes-Naming-Partner-of-the-new-stadium-of-AC-Milan-and-FC-Internazionale-Milano)

Naming rights generally give a sponsor repeated association with a place through broadcasts, tickets, maps and public discussion. They do not mean the sponsor owns the football clubs or has funded every part of construction. For readers evaluating this project, the central commercial question is how the different revenue streams support a workable operating model. That question remains distinct from whether supporters embrace the chosen name.

PRESDA's account of [PSG's transformation under QSI](https://presda.com/articles/nasser-al-khelaifi-psg-qsi-70-million-transformation/) examines another version of football's relationship with investment and commercial growth. In Milan, the stadium provides a concrete setting for that relationship: long-term infrastructure decisions can shape a club's opportunities well beyond the tenure of a coach or a playing squad.

## Sustainability promises need operational evidence

The design includes photovoltaic generation and natural ventilation. Such features describe an intention to improve how the building uses energy; they do not establish its eventual emissions, operating efficiency or whole-life environmental footprint. Those outcomes will depend on specifications, construction materials, transport patterns, maintenance and actual use. A credible assessment must follow the project from proposal into delivery. ### What supporters should watch next

The meaningful next developments will be approvals, detailed delivery commitments and information about the experience of attending matches. Ticket arrangements, accessibility, transport and the treatment of the surrounding area deserve attention alongside the facade and capacity. For now, Generali Stadium is a named and presented future home. Calling it an opened stadium would turn an ambitious plan into an event that has not happened.
