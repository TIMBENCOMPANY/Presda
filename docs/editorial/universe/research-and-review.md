## Evidence boundaries

- The hot Big Bang is supported by expansion, light-element abundances and the CMB. It is not presented as an explosion at a central point or a demonstrated explanation of absolute beginnings. NASA universe overview and Planck 2018 cosmological-parameters paper support the framework and model-dependent age near 13.8 billion years.
- Inflation is a successful theoretical framework with an unidentified mechanism. ESA Planck material supports the distinction from the established hot phase.
- ESA sources support neutral-atom formation around 380,000 years and the present microwave temperature near 2.7 K. The CMB is explicitly not a photograph of the initial event.
- NASA's How Big Is Space gives approximately 92 billion light-years across. The article explains present distance versus light-travel time and distinguishes the observable horizon from a physical edge.
- NASA stellar and solar-system sources support stellar nucleosynthesis, the Milky Way's more than 100 billion stars, the Sun's approximate 26,000-light-year galactic distance and solar-system formation around 4.6 billion years ago. ESO's GW170817 report supports neutron-star-merger heavy-element production.
- EHT/ESO's Sagittarius A* record distinguishes 2017 radio observations from the 2022 image release. The image is a reconstruction of emission around a shadow, not a photograph of a solid surface.
- ESA Planck results support approximate present energy-budget fractions 5%, 27%, 68%. These are explicitly model-based. Dark matter's particle identity and dark energy's physical explanation remain unknown.
- Berkeley Lab's 19 March 2025 DESI report supports hints of evolving dark energy in dataset combinations, not a definitive discovery or guaranteed cosmic fate.
- Nature 2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07860-9, supports spectroscopic confirmation of early luminous galaxies. NASA's January 2026 MoM-z14 report gives about 280 million years after the Big Bang. The feature does not make a fragile latest-record claim or say these observations disprove the Big Bang.
- Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-022-05269-w, supports carbon dioxide in WASP-39 b, explicitly not evidence of life. NASA astrobiology supports the absence of an established extraterrestrial-life detection.
- Cosmic futures remain conditional on the behaviour of dark energy and other physics. Accelerated expansion alone does not establish a Big Rip. A cold dilute universe is not equated with time stopping.
- NASA Voyager material supports the 14 February 1990 Pale Blue Dot photograph and Sagan's advocacy. His perspective is paraphrased without fabricated quotations.
