# Winter: editorial and production review

16 October 2026. Article 271, Science, `winter-dolphin-prosthetic-tail`.

## Evidence decisions

- CMA supports rescue on 10 December 2005 in Mosquito Lagoon and estimated age of two months. Hanger's differing age estimate is not silently substituted. Tail tissue deteriorated after loss of circulation; no invented instantaneous amputation scene.
- Winter swam without a prosthesis before its development. Hanger's 2011 account supports concern about compensatory lateral movement. The device was an external rehabilitation aid, not a permanent implant or proof of suitability for release.
- Kevin Carroll, Dan Strzempka and the roughly eighteen-month development period are attributed to Hanger. WintersGel's human applications are attributed to its developer; no universal superiority or clinical efficacy statistic is invented.
- Smithsonian's Van Phillips history supplies wider prosthetics context, not a claim that it documented Winter. Johns Hopkins and ABC supply documented human encounters. Personal inspiration is distinguished from clinical evidence and from expectations placed on disabled people.
- NWF and reporting on the sequel support the film/reality distinction. Sawyer and Hazel are fictional. Morgan Freeman's character draws on both prosthetists. No film dialogue presented as historical quotation.
- CMA's contemporaneous updates distinguish early GI concerns from the reported preliminary necropsy finding of intestinal torsion. Death: 11 November 2021, age sixteen. No invented final thoughts or unsupported prosthesis/death causation.
- NOAA supports conservation and qualified rescue advice. No instructions to attempt wildlife disentanglement.
- Thirteen source URLs shared across all editions. Two natural internal links, to Animal Testing and Laika, distinguish medical rehabilitation from deliberately imposed experimental harm.
