A vending machine normally asks for money. In Milka’s 2013 Argentine campaign, it asked people to cooperate. A purple cow and a chocolate machine stood apart, and passersby were invited to bridge the gap by holding hands. The reward was free chocolate. The idea was simple enough to understand in a few seconds: the way to get something for yourself was to connect with someone else. [InfoNegocios, contemporary Buenos Aires campaign report (2013)](https://infonegocios.info/amp/conosur/juntos-por-un-chocolate)

## A real 2013 campaign, not a new promotion

The activation was called Cadena de Ternura, or Chain of Tenderness. Contemporary trade coverage dated August 2013 credits David Buenos Aires and identifies Milka’s advertiser as Mondelēz International. This is a historical business case, not an announcement that a free-chocolate machine is operating today. Keeping the date attached to the story prevents an old campaign film from becoming a misleading current offer. [Adlatina, campaign credits (1 August 2013)](https://www.adlatina.com/campa%C3%B1as/cadena-de-ternura)

## How the human chain became the idea

The campaign film presents the participants as a human connection between the machine and the cow. Contemporary reporting describes hand-holding as the trigger for dispensing chocolate. The important distinction is between completing an activation and generating power: the people were not a human power station. The available case material does not justify inventing the installation’s voltage, electronics or detailed safety specifications. [Clube de Criação, contemporary campaign account (2 August 2013)](https://clubedecriacao.com.br/ultimas/cadena-de-ternura-2/)

The props were more than a visual joke. Production specialist Sodapasta lists its work on the cow’s modelling and mechanical construction and on an operational chocolate vending machine. Its project record dates the work to 2013 and names Argentina as the market. This production evidence supports the physical installation without turning the supplied hero into an authenticated photograph of it. [Sodapasta, original production project record](https://www.sodapasta.com/cow-vending-machine.html)

## Why participation suited the brand

As a marketing mechanism, the action joined three things: a recognisable brand symbol, a small task and an immediate reward. The cow made the installation identifiable before anyone read an explanation. The gap created a problem that required another person. The chocolate made the result tangible. Our interpretation is that this turned a message about tenderness into an action people could perform, rather than a slogan they merely watched.

## What the film cannot prove

A campaign film is evidence of how an activation was presented, not a controlled study of strangers’ relationships. It cannot by itself establish lasting friendships, a sales uplift or a return on investment. We therefore make no unsupported claims about participant totals, global reach or long-term social impact. The case is useful precisely at its proper scale: it shows a clear creative proposition and a memorable way to make participation part of the product experience.

For the product’s wider story, read our [history of chocolate](/articles/chocolate-ancient-cacao-global-obsession/). Milka’s example adds a different business lesson: a promotion can give people a reason to act together while still requiring careful evidence before anyone calls it a commercial or social success.
