## Scope and evidence

- Four complete versions: EN 2,178 words, FR 2,375, AR 1,819 and ES 1,946. Counts cover the body and headings, retaining link labels and excluding URL strings. FAQs are additional.
- Seventeen scientific or institutional references, with direct citations in the body, three localized graphics and six localized FAQs.
- The 1996 paper used the historical name Turritopsis nutricula. The article distinguishes that usage from the modern species identity and the January 2026 Zootaxa investigation.
- The 2003 Tissue and Cell study supports tissue remodeling. Its DOI parentheses are URL-encoded so the inline Markdown renderer preserves the entire link.
- Transcriptome studies from 2019 and 2021 support candidate expression pathways, not definitive cell lineage tracing.
- The 2022 PNAS genome comparison is presented alongside the 2023 critique, author reply and species-identification correction. The correction is not described as a retraction.
- The separate DNA Research resource appeared online in December 2022 and in the February 2023 issue. Its approximately 1,500 young medusae came from one clone.
- The 2024 Nature Communications work supports species-specific comparison and T. rubra reversal/diapause. No universal claim that other jellyfish cannot reverse development is made.
- The 2025 Invertebrate Biology experiment involved 102 newborn medusae; 22% failed to form cysts across its treatments. This intermediate endpoint is not treated as whole-cycle success or a wild-population mortality estimate. The author-deposited paper was checked alongside its journal/DOI record.
- Marine Biotechnology and PubMed independently identify the Ai, He and Wang paper as published on 24 August 2026, DOI `10.1007/s10126-026-10697-0`, PMID `42635858`. Its 82 RNA-sequencing samples support an association/candidate resource, not functional proof or a clinical result. The publicly available abstract supports the claims used; access to the full paper is not implied.
- The aging review is distinguished from a human clinical trial. No human age-reversal treatment, infinite measured lifespan or centuries-old individual is claimed.
