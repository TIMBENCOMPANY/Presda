# Jonathan: editorial evidence and graphic methodology

Reporting date: October 9, 2026. Living status checked against the October 7 Guinness interview and April 2 Saint Helena Government statement. Guinness's record page gives 194 in 2026, circa 1832 birth, mature arrival in 1882 and an 1882–1886 photograph. Birth year and age remain estimates, not a recorded hatch date.

Original 2026 paper: Benjamin Vaisvil et al., *Epigenetic insights into extreme longevity in the world's oldest terrestrial animal, Jonathan*, Science Advances, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adw8887. Online publication October 7; issue date October 9. Publisher page returned 403. The complete original XML was retrieved successfully through the Europe PMC full-text API for PMC13644754 and inspected, including results, discussion and limitations. Public reader link: https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC13644754. Cambridge's announcement was independently retrieved as HTML. Igenbio reproduces the abstract and lists authors.

Direct Jonathan research is documented, not inferred: cheek/saliva DNA, no permitted blood draw; Tank (36 years) reference genome; 287 genes containing unique nonsynonymous variants within the selected comparisons; methylome comparison with four Aldabras, including two older adults of unknown exact ages. Whole-genome methylation changes do occur in Jonathan. Lower disorder in some promoter regions is not whole-body youth. Paper explicitly denies sufficient data for causal inference and reports access to approximately 10% of all promoters. Proposed energy/RNA/repair feedback is hypothetical. Independent Live Science reporting includes scientists' reservations; no quotes reproduced.

2018 Quesada study concerns Lonesome George and an Aldabra tortoise, not Jonathan. 2022 da Silva Science study: roughly 75% of 52 captive turtle/tortoise species showed slow or negligible senescence, a demographic finding rather than immortality. Metabolism is not presented as a single universal cause.

Genomic origin is qualified: Jonathan clusters near Aldabra, with unusual ancestry; exact source island unresolved. Avoid assigning historically familiar hololissa identification as settled genetics.
