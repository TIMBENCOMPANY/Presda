# Plastic surgery feature verification

Reporting and source review: October 10, 2026. Category: Lifestyle.

## Evidence decisions

- The ISAPS 2025 report was released September 29, 2026. The survey estimates 17,883,610 surgical and 17,320,420 non-surgical procedures, totaling 35,204,030. These are extrapolated procedures performed by plastic surgeons, not unique people or all aesthetic providers.
- Methodology: 2,900 responding surgeons. Added procedure categories and expanded geographic coverage affect raw totals. Published comparable-category annual changes are -0.4% surgical, -15.7% non-surgical and -8.7% total. These must not be replaced by raw-total subtraction or described as uninterrupted annual growth.
- Graphics transcribe report tables for procedures (pp. 9–10), country totals (p. 83), gender (pp. 105–106) and age (p. 107). Age comparisons display only two adult bands and identify the denominator. Country figures retain rounding differences and are not safety or revenue rankings.
- The 4.0% abdominoplasty figure comes from Winocour et al., published 2015, concerning 25,478 US CosmetAssure patients in 2008–2013. It measures major complications in a selected historical population. It is not a worldwide 2026 rate or every adverse outcome.
- FDA filler and implant guidance and the BOTOX Cosmetic prescribing information support separate risk explanations. The label's qualification about recommended cosmetic doses is retained. No universal filler blindness probability or implant lifetime risk is invented.
- The 2022 joint gluteal fat grafting statement supports ultrasound verification and discusses fat embolism. Historical mortality estimates are not republished as current rates.
- Sarwer et al. (2005) reports satisfaction in a small prospective sample with attrition. Garbett et al. (2025) reviews 17 controlled prospective studies and finds limited evidence for broader psychosocial outcomes. These do not justify a universal mental-health benefit or condemnation of patients.
- Social-media associations are not described as demonstrated causation. No celebrity is diagnosed or assigned an undocumented procedure.
- CDC's June 2026 travel-complication report is a selected consultation series, not an incidence denominator. The England licensing consultation is described as planned legal work, not automatic enactment.
- Fee ranges and forecasts are distinguished from revenue. No global market valuation is calculated from US fees multiplied by worldwide procedures.
