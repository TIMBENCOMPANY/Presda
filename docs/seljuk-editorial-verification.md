# Seljuk feature: source and publishing review

Reporting date: 11 October 2026. New article 234, slug `seljuk-empire-origins-manzikert-culture-decline`.

The registry and localization files were searched for an existing standalone Seljuk article. Related references appeared in Abbasid, Byzantine, Georgian and Ottoman articles; no duplicate standalone feature was found. Existing articles are preserved.

## Editorial evidence

The four complete body versions have 61 matching blocks: EN 3,109 words, FR 3,227, AR 3,182 and ES 3,340. Counts retain citation labels but exclude URLs. Six FAQs and figure captions are additional.

Twenty-two references cover origin traditions, Chaghri, Dandanaqan, Baghdad, Alp Arslan, Manzikert, Rum, Malik Shah, Nizam al-Mulk, statecraft literature, science, architecture, economic institutions, military organization and decline. The main foundation is named academic scholarship in Encyclopaedia Iranica, the Metropolitan Museum, UNESCO, Cambridge/ARC Humanities and Edinburgh University Press. Original chronicles are approached through scholarly analysis of authorship, transmission and interpretation rather than invented quotations.

- Britannica's requested main pages were attempted but unavailable through the research tool. They are not listed as inspected sources or used as a substitute for accessible scholarship.
- Theotokis’s publicly available introduction supports the distinction between military defeat and the larger political disaster that followed. Hillenbrand’s publisher description identifies the source-comparison and memory approach; the complete purchased book is not claimed to have been read.
- Some individual Iranica biography requests timed out; accessible entries, publisher-indexed descriptions and cross-referenced academic accounts supplied the stated reign chronology. No unavailable text is quoted.
- The Great Seljuk Empire, western successors and Rum are not conflated. The article distinguishes 1157 and 1194, and avoids imposing a single exact founding date on Rum’s poorly documented formation.
- The origin tradition is explicitly qualified. Tughril’s authority at Nishapur in 1038 is separated from the imperial breakthrough at Dandanaqan in 1040.
- Romanos IV’s capture at Manzikert is established; disputed army totals, casualty rates, dialogue and detailed commanders’ motives are omitted. Anatolian transformation includes pre-1071 movements, civil wars, migration and continuing communities.
- Nizam al-Mulk’s schools are not called the invention of education. The Siyasatnama is prescriptive and textually contested, not a neutral administrative archive.
- Malik Shah’s maximum reach is not equated with uniform direct government. Golden-age language is qualified as a retrospective description.
- Iqta is described as a varied arrangement, not universally full land ownership or a simple European feudal equivalent. No GDP, modern-dollar revenue or universal tax rate is invented.
- Jerusalem’s Fatimid control in 1099 is supported by the Cambridge History of Africa chapter reproduced in the South African History Archive. The article does not identify it as a unified Seljuk capital.
- The Seljuks did not found the Ottoman dynasty. Influences are separated from political identity and genealogy.

## Image and map

No figure is authenticated as Alp Arslan, Malik Shah or another named person. The palace is not treated as evidence of an actual court.

Six localized figures: timeline, Manzikert analytical sequence, regional geographic map, key rulers table, architecture/culture comparison and military comparison. Tables and event lists are server-rendered, captioned, source-linked and keyboard accessible. The map has an accessible description and explanatory legend, with a horizontal scroll region on narrow screens. Logical spacing and bidirectional isolation support Arabic.

Map base geometry uses Natural Earth 1:110m land and lakes from its public repository, public-domain terms, and a regional equirectangular projection over longitude 25–78 and latitude 20–49. The generated file records exact data URLs. No modern administrative borders are drawn. City points use rounded geographical coordinates; they are not territorial boundaries.

The historical overlay is an original, generalized interpretation of the late eleventh-century core and extensions described in Durand-Guédy’s Malik Shah article. It is not a traced, surveyed or comprehensive border. Northern Syrian extension and Transoxianan suzerainty use different colors/styles. Rum is a separate branch marker, not painted as an imperial province. Small dependencies and precise local control are not asserted. The caption makes these limits visible.
