# Taiwan captives feature: evidence and publishing review

Reviewed 9 October 2026. Article ID 219, slug `dutch-women-taiwan-1662-zeelandia-captives`. EN, FR, AR and ES share 35 blocks, four FAQs and ten source URLs.

## Directly inspected published primary material

William Campbell, *Formosa under the Dutch* (1903), Cornell University Library scan: https://archive.org/details/cu31924023514403 . Public-domain OCR was inspected against the printed page markers. Record 116, pp. 326–327, is Commander Caeuw's day-journal entry of 21 October 1661. Its two young informants were described as enslaved or serving in slavery, not as two Dutch boys. The entry reports executions, transportation, women guarded in Provintia, Hambroek's daughter's concubinage and a sixteen-year-old girl's marriage. It also states that the boys were subsequently tortured and suspected of spying. All these provenance limits are retained. No survivor testimony from either girl is invented.

Record 117, pp. 327–328, is Joannes Kruyf's letter from Negapatam, 13 October 1662. It describes his arrival with his family and reports ministers' wives and others carried into slavery. It is a bereaved participant's account, not an independent census. No mortality totals from his letter are reproduced. This letter is distinct from the later pro-Coyett account in Part III.

Treaty transcription from Campbell pp. 455–456, https://pages.uoregon.edu/inaasim/qinglong/MingQing/Qing2.htm : dated 1 February 1662; articles 2–8 address property, provisions, archives and departure; article 9 releases; article 17 exchanges. No clause transfers women as payment. Treaty obligations are not asserted to prove universal release. No exact departure day is supplied without separate confirmation.
